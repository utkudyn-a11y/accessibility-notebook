## Personal Notes

Last week, I couldn’t write my notes to you due to… ehem… a World Cup trauma caused by the Turkish National Team. No, no, don’t take it seriously. That’s not the real reason. We attended four concerts at the İstanbul Music Festival, and most of our nights were filled with concert events, which was a dream come true for me. So, I couldn’t spend any time compiling news and articles.

By the way, we saw a great accessibility practice at the İstanbul Music Festival. Most of the concerts were classical music concerts. And there were printed concert programs distributed just before the event. They are important for reading about the details of the composer, the pieces, and the movements. But unfortunately, most of the time, they are printed materials, and I cannot read the small font size. Thankfully, [İKSV](https://www.linkedin.com/company/iksv/) placed a QR code on the printed concert brochures, so I could read all the details from a web page easily by using my phone. An easy, affordable and impactful accessibility solution. Thank you İKSV!

## News

### France’s Major Court Decision Supporting Digital Accessibility Under The EAA

Source: [Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/frances-major-court-decision-supporting-digital-accessibility-under-the-eaa/)

Finally, we have seen a concrete legal decision for the EAA from France after a year. In November 2025, four of France’s largest retailers, including Carrefour, were taken to court by disability rights organisations for failing to make their online grocery services accessible. Subsequently, “the Tribunal judiciaire de Caen (Normandie) ordered Carrefour France to make its online commerce services fully accessible to people with disabilities. The court has given the organization six months to comply, with fines to accrue for each day of delay.” Happy to see the EAA in action and waiting for more concrete steps.

## In Detail: Is the Use Of Accessibility Overlays For Dyslexia Meaningful?

The topic of accessibility overlays is still the most controversial issue in the field. Overlays are extensions that cause problems in terms of usability, legal compliance, and technical capabilities. I previously wrote a detailed article explaining this topic, and I still stand by it. “[Are Accessibility Overlays Really Meaningful?”](https://accessibilityperson.blogspot.com/2025/11/are-accessibility-overlays-really.html)

In summary, accessibility overlays are extension-based solutions. They try to modify elements by integrating into your website and they generally cannot be very successful. Furthermore, the customizations mentioned in the Funka Foundation article—such as character spacing, line spacing, text size, font, contrast, and zoom customizations—are already features that users can control directly from their own devices or browsers.

The important thing is to ensure that when line spacing is increased, the content does not break or disappear. Or, to pay attention to contrast rules. In short, your content must be inherently accessible from the start. That’s the key thing.
